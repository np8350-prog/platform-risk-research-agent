**How they integrate:**- **Native integrations** with databases (PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, etc.) and APIs (REST, GraphQL)- **REST APIs** for custom connections- **SDK** and JavaScript-based customization- **Self-hosted** via Docker containers- **No-code/low-code** visual builder with drag-and-drop widgets**Export and leaving:**- Appsmith allows **exporting apps** (JSON format) and importing them- **Datasource configuration fields can be lost** during export/import/forking—a known issue where URL, content-type, and headers are not preserved- **Data export** (table data) requires building a backend API- **Self-hosted users** retain full control over their data since Appsmith does not store it**Known integration failures:**- Datasource fields lost during forking/export/import (Issue #20545, #29399)- Permission issues with partial import/export (Issue #32048)- CVE-2026-55455 (SSRF) involved insufficient host denylist in REST/GraphQL datasource plugins**Dependencies:**- **OpenAI** for Appsmith AI features- **AWS** for Appsmith Cloud hosting- **Third-party databases and APIs** as datasources (customer-configured)**Lock-in risk: Low to Moderate.** Appsmith's open-source nature and self-hosting option significantly reduce lock-in. Since Appsmith does **not store** user data processed through apps, there is minimal data lock-in. The primary lock-in is **workflow/configuration lock-in**—exporting complex apps with widgets, queries, and datasource configurations requires effort, and known issues with datasource field loss during export add friction. However, the ability to self-host and export apps in JSON format makes switching to alternatives (Retool, etc.) feasible, though not trivial.
