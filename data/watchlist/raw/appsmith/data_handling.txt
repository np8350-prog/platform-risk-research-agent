•	General Appsmith Cloud data: Appsmith's privacy policy states that it does not store User data processed as part of its Services and describes such data as transient, subject to service-specific documentation. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	Sensitive data: Appsmith states that sensitive user information is encrypted within its infrastructure. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	Third parties: Appsmith states that data may be accessible to third-party technical service providers, hosting providers, IT companies and other processors required to operate the service. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	Data-sale policy: Appsmith states that it does not sell personal data and does not exchange personal data for monetary value or advertising purposes. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	Authentication data: Appsmith supports third-party authentication through services such as Google OAuth and GitHub OAuth; the privacy policy states that processing occurs through those providers and identifies the United States as the processing location for those services. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	Google data: When users explicitly authorize Google integrations, Appsmith may access Gmail, Contacts, Drive, Docs and related data depending on the scopes granted. Appsmith states that it accesses only the data necessary for configured functionality. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	Google-data storage: Appsmith states that it does not ordinarily store Google emails, Drive file contents or other Google personal data on its servers unless the user explicitly saves that information as part of an Appsmith application. OAuth tokens are stored securely and encrypted to maintain authorized connections. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	AI processing: Appsmith AI uses OpenAI APIs for chat completions and embeddings. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	AI chat data: Appsmith states that Appsmith AI chat-completion interactions are processed in real time through OpenAI APIs and that OpenAI response data is not stored, logged or retained by Appsmith. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	AI file uploads: Appsmith states that uploaded files are processed to generate embeddings using OpenAI's embedding API, with the resulting embeddings stored in a PostgreSQL database; it states that the original files/content are not stored after processing. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	AI training: Appsmith states that Google user data is not used to develop, improve or train generalized/non-personalized AI or ML models. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy) For Appsmith AI generally, the privacy policy does not make an equally broad, standalone statement that no Appsmith data of any kind can ever be used for model improvement; it instead describes limited anonymized usage/analytics data and OpenAI processing. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	OpenAI dependency: Appsmith explicitly states that OpenAI may process Appsmith AI data according to OpenAI's privacy policy and that Appsmith does not control OpenAI's data-processing policies. (https://www.appsmith.com/privacy-policy)
	•	Internal Appsmith data: In a 2024 GDPR article, Appsmith states that its own user data is stored in several systems, including internal MongoDB, a Segment warehouse backed by PostgreSQL, Mixpanel and Mailmodo. (https://www.appsmith.com/blog/automating-gdpr-data-deletion-requests-with-appsmith-workflows)
	•	Self-hosting: Appsmith can be deployed on a customer's own infrastructure, including local/private servers, Docker/Kubernetes environments and AWS deployments. (https://docs.appsmith.com/) (https://www.appsmith.com/integration/open-ai)
	•	Data residency advantage: With self-hosting, the customer controls the infrastructure on which Appsmith runs, which can substantially reduce SaaS data-residency exposure. Appsmith explicitly positions self-hosting as giving organizations control over sensitive internal data. (https://www.appsmith.com/workflows)
	•	Overall data-handling assessment: LOWER risk for self-hosted deployments; MEDIUM risk for Appsmith Cloud/AI. The main concern for cloud/AI use is the third-party processing chain, particularly OpenAI, analytics providers and authentication providers. For sensitive workloads, self-hosting materially improves control.
