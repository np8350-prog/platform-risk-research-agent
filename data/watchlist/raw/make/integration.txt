**How they integrate:**- **Visual drag-and-drop builder** with 3,000+ pre-built integrations- **REST API** with endpoints for managing scenarios, teams, and data stores- **Webhooks** for real-time triggers- **SDK** for building custom apps and integrations- **MCP (Model Context Protocol)** support for AI agent integration- **Prompt-based** and **code** options alongside visual building**Export and leaving:**- Workflow logic is described as **"moderately portable"**—the logic translates to other platforms, but **integrations** (specific connectors and configurations) are the main friction point.- Users can download **input/output bundles** from scenario history for debugging and potentially for migration.- No built-in "export all scenarios" feature is prominently documented; migration typically requires manual re-creation of workflows on alternative platforms.**Known integration failures:**- **Google disconnections** are a frequently cited pain point- **Discord integration** failed for 4 weeks before being fixed- **Facebook Leads API** issues affecting data and payloads- **Third-party queuing tool** hardware difficulties caused a 15-minute outage**Dependencies:**- **AWS** for cloud infrastructure and hosting- **Third-party apps and APIs** that users connect to (3,000+ integrations)- No direct dependency on OpenAI or specific AI providers—Make is model-agnostic and supports multiple AI services**Lock-in risk: Moderate.** The main lock-in factors are:- **Workflow configuration**—complex scenarios with many modules and custom logic require significant effort to rebuild elsewhere- **Integration-specific configurations**—each connected app has its own authentication and field mappings- **Data stores**—Make's built-in data storage would need to be migrated- However, Make's **open API** and **SDK** provide some escape routes, and the platform's visual nature means logic is somewhat portable. Switching away would be **time-consuming and costly** for organizations with extensive, complex automations, but not impossible.
