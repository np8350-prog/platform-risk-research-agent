•	Data processed: Make's DPA states that the service processes user-account information such as name, username/ID, contact details, log/protocol data, plus additional personal data depending on how the customer uses the service. (https://www.make.com/data-processing-agreement.pdf)
	•	Workflow data: Make's core function necessarily processes data passed through customer scenarios to connect and automate third-party applications. The DPA describes the service as integrating and automating third-party applications, websites and services maintained by the controller. (https://www.make.com/data-processing-agreement.pdf)
	•	Usage telemetry: Celonis/Make's privacy notice says it may collect application-usage information including applications and versions used, user interactions, searches, pages/files viewed, data sources queried, visualizations built, system configuration, workflow steps, operations, queries, logs and timestamps. (https://www.make.com/en/privacy-notice)
	•	Device data: The privacy notice also identifies device type, approximate location derived from IP, browser/OS information, IP address, access times, duration and related identifiers as data that may be collected. (https://www.make.com/en/privacy-notice)
	•	Data-center location: Customers choose the organization's data-center location when creating the Make organization. Make currently supports United States and European Union data centers. (https://help.make.com/organizations)
	•	Important residency limitation: Make states that the data-center location cannot be changed after the organization is created. (https://help.make.com/organizations)
	•	Default retention: Make states that log data is stored for 30 days by default. Enterprise customers can obtain extended storage periods. (https://www.make.com/en/security)
	•	Data deletion: Make's GDPR page states that once personal data is no longer required, including after subscription termination, customers can request deletion of all personal data. (https://www.make.com/en/privacy-and-gdpr)
	•	Confidential-data option: Make has historically provided a Data is confidential option that prevents processed scenario data from being stored on Make's servers. A Make community response describes this option as ensuring that data processed by a scenario is not stored and therefore cannot be displayed after execution. (https://community.make.com/t/information-security-compliance-soc2-iso27001-hipaa-etc/8052/3)
	•	Encryption at rest: Make states that it uses AES-256 full-disk encryption and AWS KMS for cryptographic-key management. (https://www.make.com/en/security)
	•	Encryption in transit: Make states that network communications use TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3 with AES-256 encryption. (https://www.make.com/en/security)
	•	Enterprise isolation: Make states that its Enterprise platform runs in a separately managed AWS environment isolated from self-service cloud customers. (https://www.make.com/en/security)
	•	Subprocessors: Make's contractual documentation includes a current subprocessor list and technical/organizational measures as part of its data-privacy documentation. (https://www.make.com/en/terms-and-conditions)
	•	Third-party infrastructure: Make's status page demonstrates dependency on third-party infrastructure such as Cloudflare, whose API outage caused Make scenario failures in June 2026. (https://status.make.com/)
	•	AI data: Make now provides AI agents and AI modules, and some AI functionality uses external model providers. For example, Make documents integrations with OpenAI and Google Vertex AI. (https://www.make.com/en/integrations/google-vertex-ai/google-forms) (https://www.make.com/en/integrations/google-docs/openai-gpt-3)
	•	Make AI Content Extractor: Make's built-in AI Content Extractor states that its data is not stored or used for AI training, while the underlying modules may use Microsoft or OpenAI depending on the extraction function. (https://apps.make.com/make-ai-extractors)
	•	General AI-training caveat: I did not find a single current Make policy stating that all data processed through every AI integration can never be used for model training. Instead, training/retention treatment depends on the particular AI provider/module. No URL available for a blanket no-training claim.
	•	Overall data-handling assessment: MEDIUM risk, configurable to lower risk. EU data residency, encryption, configurable logging/retention and Enterprise isolation are strong controls. The main risk is that Make is an orchestration layer connecting many third-party systems, so the effective data-processing chain depends heavily on the applications and AI providers used in each scenario.
